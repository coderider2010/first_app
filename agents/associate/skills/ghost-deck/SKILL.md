# Ghost Deck

## Why ghost first

A ghost deck is the storyline forced to confront slide logic before any analysis is
spent. Its two jobs: (1) expose where the storyline doesn't actually hang together, and
(2) generate the module list — every placeholder exhibit that no commissioned module
will fill is a gap the EM needs to see. You are not decorating an answer; you are
debugging one. A ghost deck that "works" on a broken storyline is a failed ghost deck.

## The two logics

**Horizontal logic**: read only the titles, top to bottom. They must form a complete,
persuasive argument on their own — a skeptical executive reading titles alone should
arrive at the recommendation and know why. Test: paste the titles into a paragraph. If
it reads as a non-sequitur anywhere, the storyline has a hole *there*, and no exhibit
will patch it.

**Vertical logic**: each slide's body must prove exactly its title — not support it,
not relate to it, *prove* it. One claim per slide. If proving the title needs two
exhibits with different messages, it is two slides. If the title can't name what would
prove it, the title is a topic, not a claim — rewrite it.

Titles are claims with direction and magnitude: "EU volumes decline ~4%/yr, driven by
Germany" — never "EU volume overview". If you cannot yet commit to direction, the title
states the question being tested and the slide is flagged OPEN.

## Dot-dash discipline

Before drawing any slide, write the argument as dot-dash: each dot a slide title, each
dash beneath it the evidence that slide will show. Iterate with the EM at dot-dash
level — it is 10x cheaper to restructure than a drawn skeleton. Only when the dot-dash
survives does it become slides.

## Placeholder rules — the integrity core of this skill

- Placeholders show the **shape** of the expected evidence (chart form, axes, units),
  never invented **values**. Grey bars, labeled axes, no numbers.
- Every placeholder carries three tags: expected direction ("we believe declining"),
  confidence (H/M/L, per the storyline branch it comes from), and the module that will
  fill it — or **`[NO MODULE]`** in red if none is commissioned.
- Anything speculative is watermarked ILLUSTRATIVE. A ghost slide must be impossible
  to mistake for a finding, even screenshotted out of context. Assume the worst: ghost
  decks leak into client hands. Design every page to be harmless when it does.
- **Never let the ghost harden.** The deck states hypotheses; when real modules return,
  evidence overwrites the ghost — the ghost never argues back. If you notice reluctance
  to change a title because the flow reads well, escalate to the EM: that is the
  storyline steering the evidence, the firm's named worst failure.

## Structure defaults

Executive summary first (the whole argument on one page, pyramid form). Then situation
→ complication → resolution ordering, or the EM's tree order. Appendix is where proof
lives that the argument references but the meeting doesn't need. Default to fewer
slides: a 12-slide deck whose titles carry the argument beats a 40-slide tour. Every
slide past the argument's minimum is surface area for questions that derail the meeting.

## Output

1. The skeleton deck (per firm template).
2. **The gap list** — every `[NO MODULE]` placeholder, every OPEN title, every
   horizontal-logic hole found, delivered to the EM as a module-commissioning queue.
   This list is half the value of the exercise; omitting it halves the skill.

## Failure modes

- **Topic titles** ("Market overview") — the deck stops arguing.
- **The confident ghost** — placeholders that look like findings; direction tags
  missing; a partner presents a grey bar as fact.
- **Vertical mismatch** — body proves something adjacent to the title; readers sense
  the wobble before they can name it.
- **Deck bloat** — slides that exist because analysis exists. The storyline, not the
  work done, determines what appears.
- **Ghost lock-in** — the skeleton survives contact with contradicting evidence.

## Handoff

Tag each slide with its hypothesis-tree branch and module IDs so the EM's storyline
versioning can diff decks against tree changes automatically.
