# Module 02: What the price implies (reverse DCF)

**Engagement:** Project Hub II (what is HubSpot worth per share?) · **Module:** 02 implied expectations · **From:** Associate · **To:** EM
**As of:** 2026-10-12 · **Price date:** close of 9 Oct 2026 · **Latest reported period:** Q2 2026 (TTM to 30 Jun 2026)
**Confidence:** **Medium.** The bridge and base figures are facts, but the result depends on three judgment inputs (discount rate, terminal growth, headline growth path). Each one moves the implied margin by 1–4 points. None of them changes the conclusion.

## Answer

**At $228.30 per share (NYSE close, 9 Oct 2026), the market is pricing in roughly 10% annual revenue growth for 10 years and a 15.6% long-run FCF-after-SBC margin** (estimate; 10.5% discount rate, 3.0% terminal growth). The price does need margins to rise, from 4.2% today to almost four times that level. But **it does not need fast growth, and it does not need HubSpot to reach peer margins**. The implied margin is 7 to 18 points below every peer the brief names (Autodesk 22.5%, Salesforce 26.2%, Intuit 30.6%, Adobe 33.3%). If HubSpot only reached the lowest peer's margin, the price would be justified at about 6% annual growth. That growth rate is roughly a third of its last three years (21.8% CAGR). **The working answer for Branch B ("fast growth *and* rising margins toward peers") is only half supported.** The rising-margins half holds. The fast-growth and toward-peers parts do not. On this evidence the price looks undemanding rather than stretched. See Escalation 1.

1. **The price needs margins to quadruple, but only to about half the peer median.** Moving from 4.2% to 15.6% is a real climb. For example, FCF before SBC rising from 19.1% to about 27% while SBC falls from 14.9% to about 11% of revenue would get there. Both moves are already under way: SBC was 19.9% of revenue in FY2023 and 14.1% in Q2 2026 (module 01). The end state would still be below Autodesk, the weakest peer, on both measures.
2. **The price does not need fast growth.** The headline 10% CAGR is consistent with guidance fading. A path that starts at the Q3 2026 guide (+14%) and declines in a straight line to 6% in year 10 has a 10.0% CAGR and needs 15.3%, almost the same as the constant-10% row. Our own record is 21.8% CAGR (FY2022–25), but it is decelerating: 25% → 21% → 19% → +14% guided for Q3 2026, with NRR slipping to about 102% (module 01). Ten years at 10% takes revenue to $8.9B, 2.6x today. That is a large business, but Autodesk is at $7.2B and Adobe at $23.8B today.
3. **Read the other way, peer-level margins justify the price at low single-digit or mid-single-digit growth.** At the base discount rate the implied growth rate is 5.8% at Autodesk's 22.5%, 3.1% at the peer median of 28.4%, and 1.3% at Adobe's 33.3% (estimates, `implied_growth` in `reverse_dcf.py`). A market expecting both fast growth and peer margins would be paying much more than $228. EV is 3.1x TTM revenue and 16x pre-SBC FCF, but 74x FCF after SBC. The thin after-SBC margin is the whole debate.
4. **The conclusion survives every sensitivity I ran** (headline row, 10% growth, unless stated). At a ±1 point discount rate the implied margin is 12.9–18.5%. Terminal growth of 2.5% / 3.5% / 4.0% (the cap) gives 16.4% / 14.8% / 14.0%. The diluted-share range from module 01 (51.33–52.69M) moves it by −0.4 to +0.1 point (15.2–15.7%). Adding the announced $70M restructuring cash cost to EV gives 15.7%. Mid-year discounting would lower every cell by about 0.7 point. Even at an 11.5% discount rate and 6% growth (26.0%), the implied margin stays below the peer median. To make the price "demanding" against peers at 10% growth you would need a discount rate above about 12.8% (13.0% gives 23.3%), or growth below about 6% at the base rate.

## Escalations to the EM

1. **Finding against the working answer (highest priority, not softened).** Branch B says the price assumes fast growth *and* rising margins toward peers. The price implies rising margins, but only to about 55% of the peer median, and modest growth (about 10%, or about 6% at the lowest peer margin). The verifier flagged this: "headline margin 15.6% is below every peer (lowest 22.5%): the price looks undemanding." This shifts the storyline question from "can HubSpot justify an expensive price?" to "why does the market doubt HubSpot will ever earn mature-software margins after SBC?" The likely answers are hypotheses, not findings: AI disruption of seat-based SMB CRM, slowing NRR, and SBC staying high. The stock fell from a $577 average buyback price in Q2 2025 to $228 (module 01). Modules 03 and 04 should test these directly. I have not changed the storyline; that is your call.
2. **The EV bridge follows the brief and leaves out the announced restructuring.** $65–75M of mostly cash charges were announced on 1 Oct 2026 (module 01, S10). The skill would add this as a debt-like item. The brief defines EV as price × shares + debt − cash, so I followed the brief and show the restructuring as a sensitivity (+0.1 point). Please confirm the convention for 03.
3. **The pins mix dates.** The price is from 9 Oct; shares and cash are from 30 Jun. Q3 buybacks (a $1.0B program was authorized on 3 Aug) lower both shares and cash. At near-market prices the effect is roughly neutral per share, but the Q3 10-Q (early November) will move EV. I have not adjusted for it.
4. **Intuit's FY2026 cash flow is unusually strong.** OCF rose from 6,207 to 8,838, including a +1,279 deferred-tax swing. On FY2025 its margin would be 21.9%, not 30.6%. With FY2025 the peer range becomes 21.9–33.3% and the median about 24%. The conclusion is unchanged (15.6% is still below every peer), but the brief fixed "latest fiscal year", so I report 30.6%.

## Confidence

**Medium.** The facts (price, bridge, base figures, peer filings) are high confidence and re-run from source. The judgment inputs move the headline margin between about 13% and 18.5%. Across that whole range it stays below every peer, so the qualitative answer ("rising margins, but not to peer levels; growth not required to be fast") is robust. **Top two things that would change the answer:** (1) a structurally lower margin ceiling for HubSpot, for example SBC staying at about 15% of revenue.
