# Module 04: Peer comparison

**Engagement:** Project Hub · **Module:** 04-peer-comparison · **From:** Associate · **To:** EM
**As of:** 2026-10-11 (prices: 2026-10-09 close; financials: latest 10-Q/10-K, periods ending Jun–Jul 2026) · **Confidence:** Low

## Answer

**Listed peers imply $167–$454 per share for HubSpot (basis: the two plain-median methods), against today's $228.30 (close 2026-10-09).** The two methods disagree by 2.7x, and that gap is the finding. On revenue, the median peer multiple (6.26x) is worth $454. The market prices HubSpot at 2.95x revenue, below every one of the seven peers, including 8–10% growers. On free cash flow after SBC, the median peer multiple (49.1x) is worth only $167. HubSpot converts 4.2% of revenue to FCF after SBC; the peer median is 14.9%. The price sits between the two views. The market is valuing HubSpot as a fast grower with weak cash conversion. The growth-and-margin adjustment (rule of 40) explains almost none of the peer multiples (r² 0.045), so it cannot settle which view is right.

**Why the range basis is "methods", not the regression band:** the skill uses the regression band only when r² ≥ 0.3. Here r² is 0.045, so the band ($249–$733) is not used. The checker's one flag ("fit is weak … lean on the plain medians") is the reason the range runs from median to median and the $491 regression value is left out.

1. **On revenue, HubSpot is the cheapest company in the set.** Its 2.95x EV/revenue is below DocuSign's 3.79x (8.6% growth) and Box's 4.53x (9.6% growth). Yet its 21.1% TTM growth is second only to ServiceNow's 22.2%. A buyer paying the peer median revenue multiple would pay roughly twice today's price.
2. **On cash, HubSpot is expensive, and SBC is not the reason.** HubSpot's after-SBC FCF margin is 4.2%; the peer median is 14.9%, a 10.7-point gap. HubSpot's SBC is 14.9% of revenue, *below* the peer median of 16.2%, so SBC explains none of the gap. The gap is in cash generation *before* SBC: 19.1% for HubSpot against a peer median of 31.1%, a 12.0-point shortfall. That is why the EV/FCF method gives $167. *Hypothesis, not tested:* HubSpot's SMB base pays more often monthly, so it collects less cash in advance (deferred revenue) than enterprise vendors with annual upfront billing. Capitalized software of 4.0% of revenue is a second factor (fact).
3. **The rule of 40 does not explain these multiples.** Okta (12.8x) and Twilio (8.0x) score about 25–26%, the same as Workday (4.5x) and HubSpot. Their premiums reflect something the score doesn't capture. As a *sensitivity only* (the peer set is fixed by the rules and is not changed here): without Okta and Twilio, r² rises to 0.43 and the regression gives $366. Without Twilio alone, the medians give $151–$395.
4. **The main reason HubSpot deserves a lower multiple than the median peer is weaker cash conversion, plus decelerating growth.** Its pre-SBC FCF margin is 12 points below the median. Its trailing 21% growth overstates the run-rate: the fact pack shows guidance implying about 14% in H2 2026. Growth is the one argument for a higher multiple, and it is fading. That supports a revenue multiple below the 6.26x median. It also leaves the after-SBC FCF view ($167) as a floor that assumes HubSpot never closes the margin gap.

## Escalations to the EM

1. **Points against the working hypothesis (highest priority).** The hypothesis says today's price assumes HubSpot keeps growing quickly while expanding margins. Peers show the market pricing HubSpot *below* every peer on revenue, including companies growing at 8–10%. On a revenue basis the price embeds less than peers' expectations, not more. Only on after-SBC cash does HubSpot look richly priced (70.8x vs the 49.1x median). Cash conversion, not growth, is what the price is discounting.
2. **The two independent methods disagree by 2.7x ($167 vs $454), well beyond the 30% tolerance.** I have not averaged them. The midpoint ($310) is not an answer, and the gap should go to the EM unresolved.
3. **Independence disclosure.** As instructed, I read `docs/engagements/hubspot-value.md`. It contains one line summarizing Module 2's result: the price implies ~9% growth and a ~17% margin. It says nothing about Module 3's DCF value. I read no Module 02/03 files. Peer selection followed the brief's rules mechanically, and every decision was recorded before any price was collected.
4. **Material items the brief didn't ask about.** ServiceNow made acquisitions in H1 2026 (goodwill +$6.3B; debt from $1.5B to $7.5B), so part of its 22.2% growth is inorganic. Autodesk closed MaintainX ($3.6B) on 2026-08-03. Because that is after its balance sheet, it sits outside both its EV bridge and its TTM revenue, which keeps the two consistent. Twilio booked a $992M non-cash tax benefit in Q2 2026; this does not affect FCF. Box's $500M convertible preferred is counted as debt.
5. **Judgment call on Twilio (rule 2).** 74% of its revenue is usage-based cloud fees, not subscription. I included it because the rule reads "subscription *or cloud software*", and I decided this before seeing any multiple. Excluding it moves the medians to $151–$395.

## Confidence: Low

The arithmetic ties: `valuation_check.py` reports no errors and one flag, which is addressed above. But the two methods disagree by 2.7x and the growth adjustment explains almost nothing, so the answer depends on a judgment the data cannot make: which multiple the market should apply to a fast grower with weak cash conversion.

**Top 2 things that would change the answer:**
1. **Evidence that HubSpot's pre-SBC cash conversion can close toward the peer ~31%** (for example, the Analyst Day margin targets from 2026-09-17, not on EDGAR). That would move the defensible value toward the EV/revenue view ($454). Evidence that the gap is structural (SMB billing) pins it near the FCF view ($167–$236).
2. **The two high-multiple peers (Okta 12.8x, Twilio 8.0x).** Together they lift the median EV/revenue value from $336 to $454. Q3 2026 results (early November) will also refresh every TTM figure, including HubSpot's guided slowdown to ~14%.
