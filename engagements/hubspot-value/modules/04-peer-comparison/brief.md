# Brief 04: Peer comparison

**Engagement:** Project Hub · **From:** EM
**Skill:** `benchmarking` (stub: flag it, then follow the charter)
**Depends on:** Brief 01. **Do not read Brief 03's output.** The two valuation methods
must stay independent.

## Question
What do listed software peers suggest HubSpot is worth per share?

## Why it matters
It gives a second, independent view of value. If it lands far from the DCF, the gap
itself is a finding.

## Method
1. **Write the selection rules before looking at any multiples**, so the peer set isn't
   chosen to give a convenient answer. Examples: subscription software, selling mainly to
   businesses, similar size and growth. Then pick 5–8 peers that fit.
2. For each peer, record enterprise value, revenue (trailing and forward where available),
   revenue growth, free cash flow margin, EV/revenue and EV/free cash flow, each with its
   source and date.
3. Adjust for growth. A faster-growing company deserves a higher multiple, so compare
   like with like, for example by plotting EV/revenue against growth.
4. Apply the adjusted multiples to HubSpot to get a value range per share.

## Sources
Peers' SEC filings and share prices (with date and source). Free sources only. If a
forward estimate isn't freely available, use trailing figures and say so.

## Output
`memo.md`, answer first: a value range per share next to today's price, followed by the
peer table, the selection rules and the main reason HubSpot deserves a higher or lower
multiple than the median peer.
